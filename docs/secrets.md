# Things to find (spoilers)

> **Spoilers ahead.** Everything on this page is left out of `help` on purpose,
> so that it can be stumbled on. The scenery drops the odd hint (listed with each
> one below), and the rest is for finding. If you'd rather find them yourself,
> stop reading here. If you like knowing exactly what's what, read on.
>
> The plain rules (who can do what, limits and timings) are in
> [rules.md](rules.md), and the everyday things are in [playing.md](playing.md).

Hints come one at a time, now and then, from whatever's around you, and never
the same one twice in a visit. Nothing here pipes up at someone who's stepped
away.

## At the seaside

**Sandcastles.** On sand, `make a sandcastle`. Anyone can add to it with the
same command: each go adds something (a moat with a seaweed drawbridge, three
wobbly turrets, a courtyard just the right size for a crab…), up to six bits, and `look` shows who built it. Every few hours (two to
four) the tide comes in and smooths every sandcastle on the island back into
sand. *Hint: "The damp sand here would make a lovely sandcastle."*

**Messages in bottles.** By the sea, `bottle To whoever finds this: have a
lovely day!` (or `throw a message in a bottle …`), up to 200 characters. It
drifts for somewhere between two hours and a day and a bit, then washes up on a
beach at least twelve steps away. Anyone who comes within three steps sees
something glinting at the water's edge, and `open bottle` on the spot reads it.
You can only have one bottle at sea at a time. Bottles from people you've muted
never reach you. *Hint: "An empty bottle bobs past on the waves. Someone ought
to put a note in one."*

**Skimming stones.** Next to a pond or the sea, `skim` (or `skip stones`). How
far it goes is described in words, never a score, and something watches: a duck
looks deeply offended, a seal pops up, a crab gives it a slow clap. *Hint: "A
flat, smooth stone catches your eye. Perfect for skimming."*

**The seagull.** Very occasionally, if you're on the beach carrying food, a
seagull starts circling. You have about fifteen seconds to `shoo` it. If you
don't, it swoops down and steals one bit of food from your satchel. It won't
come for the same person more than once every forty minutes.

## Weather toys

**Kites.** On a breezy day, on open dry ground (not in the woods), `fly a kite`.
You get a random kite (red, rainbow-striped, dragon, patchwork…), and it follows
you about as you walk, swooping and looping now and then. Anyone within four
steps can see it. Walk near trees and a gust may tangle it in the branches;
someone else nearby can `untangle` it for you. `reel in` brings it down, and it
drifts gently back into your arms if the wind drops. *Hint: "The wind tugs at your sleeves.
Proper kite weather."*

**Puddles.** When it's raining, on dry ground, `jump in a puddle` (or `splash`,
`stomp`). Anyone with you gets splashed. *Hint: "Puddles are gathering
everywhere. Very jumpable."*

**Snow.** All winter: `make a snow angel` (they stay until fresh snow fills them,
and several people's lie side by side) and `make a snowman`, which anyone can add
to (a carrot nose, a scarf of knotted seaweed, three shell buttons… up to six
bits). Snowmen last the
whole winter and melt when spring comes. *Hints: "The snow here is just right for
a snowman." "The snow looks soft enough to lie down and make a snow angel in."*

## The hills

On a hill or the stony heights:

- `look out` shows the whole island from up there: who's about and where, kites
  bobbing on the wind, smoke from anyone's campfire, the other islands hazy on
  the horizon, and the island map.
- A `shout` comes echoing back off the hills: "…hello… hello… hello…".
- `roll a stone` sets one bounding down the slope, one way or another. It might
  plop into a pond, thud into the trees, bury itself in the sand, or roll to a
  stop at someone's feet (and they'll hear about it).

*Hints: "The wind's fresh up here, and the view must be something." "Up here,
your voice would carry for miles." "A round stone sits at the top of the slope,
just asking to be rolled."*

## Things for two

Each of these works alone, and is nicer with company. Residents don't count as
company.

- `rock paper scissors` (or `rps`) starts a game with everyone standing with
  you. Each person types `rock`, `paper` or `scissors` in secret, and when
  everyone has, the throws come out together. With more than two, everyone who
  threw the winning one wins, and all the same or one of everything is a draw.
  People who arrive can join by choosing, `reveal` shows what's in so far, and a
  game nobody finishes is forgotten after ten minutes. Nothing is scored or
  kept. *Hint: "Two's company. You could settle just about anything with a game
  of rock, paper, scissors."*
- `picnic` spreads a checked blanket. If there's food in your satchel, one bit is
  unpacked and shared round. Not in the rain, and not in the water. *Hint:
  "What a spot for a picnic."*
- `umbrella` in the rain, and everyone with you huddles underneath. *Hint: "The
  rain's coming down. An umbrella's just big enough for two."*
- `stargaze` on a clear night, and someone spots a constellation that looks just
  like a teapot, pouring, or an owl wearing a hat. *Hint: "The stars are out,
  bright and thick."*
- `high five Sam` (or just `high five` with one other person there). Smack.

The hat game (`ask` / `answer`) is the practical cousin of rock, paper,
scissors, and it is in the help: see [playing.md](playing.md).

## Dressing up and looking at people

`wear a daisy chain` (anything you like, up to forty letters): everyone who
looks where you are sees it. `take off` takes it off. `look Poppy` looks at
someone standing with you: what they're wearing and whether they're flying a
kite. *Hint (in a meadow): "Daisies everywhere. You could make a daisy chain and
wear it."*

## Wild animals have their own lives

The wild animals (see Companions in [playing.md](playing.md)) don't wait
where they turn up. Each wanders its own kind of ground a step at a time (the
rabbit hops, the hedgehog trundles, the turtle plods, very slowly), now and
then drawn towards whoever's close by, so they come and go past you. With
someone watching, they get on with their day: a fox washes a paw, a duckling
upends itself for weed. They nose at things people have left about, size each
other up, and touch noses with a companion of their own kind. `pat` (or
`stroke`) a wild one and it might let you, or might not. Night creatures turn
in at dawn, and day ones at dusk. Animals (and companions) keep quiet for
anyone who's stepped away.

Each island has creatures of its own, found nowhere else (a companion can
come home with you, though):

- **Home Island**: a barn owl and a curlew at night, wailing in the dark, a
  wombat bulldozing through the grass, and a kookaburra laughing in the trees
  by day.
- **Driftwood Isle**: a seal lolling on the sand, a pelican looking important,
  and little penguins waddling up from the water after dark.
- **Mossy Holm**: a badger and a possum at night, a red squirrel by day, and a
  koala, mostly asleep.
- **Ember Rock**: a mountain goat on impossible ledges, a salamander under
  the rocks at night, a kangaroo and an echidna.

Companions have indoor manners too. In a room they leave the leaves and clover
outside: a fox curls up in the warmest corner, a rabbit flops over on its
side, a turtle finds the warm patch by the window. Put a cushion, rug or
anything soft in there and they'll settle on it now and then.

## Island folk

Each island has one or two folk on it at a time: scripted characters (not
AI, and not people) with names drawn from a long list and a trade. There's
a beachcomber, a gardener, a painter, a lamplighter, a fiddler, a knitter, a
birdwatcher, a kite-maker, a baker and a storyteller. Somebody new rows in
now and then, potters about for anything from a few hours to a couple of
days, then heads for the dock and rows off again.

- They wander their island, to the kind of ground their trade likes (the
  beachcomber to the sand, the painter up the hills), and sometimes over to
  a friend or to whoever's about. They never go into anyone's home or
  building.
- They keep island hours: up at dawn, and asleep under a blanket at night.
- With someone there, they get on with things: the knitter counts stitches,
  the fiddler plays an old tune (which plays in the browser). Two who meet
  have a little chat. They say hello to animals and companions.
- Now and then they say something to whoever's standing with them, but only
  to someone who's about (never to someone who's stepped away), and to the
  same person at most once every ten minutes. Some of them hand over a
  little something, once a day at most.
- Now and then they gossip about the other islands: a find that grows only
  over there (one you haven't found yet) or a creature you'd only meet there.
- They answer if you say hello, or say their name, or `talk to Bramble`.
  `look Bramble` looks at them, and `give daisy to Bramble` gives them
  something, which they keep. Asleep, only their name stirs them.
  In the browser, someone standing with one who's awake gets a 👋 hello
  button in the row above the box, and their name in the look text taps to
  look at them. That's all the row offers: gifts, parties and the rest stay
  for people to find.
- They make small things that don't last (a painting drying on an easel, a
  lantern on a pole for the night, a tray of buns cooling on a stump) and
  tidy them away again after a few hours. One thing each at a time, eight
  on the whole island at most, never in a crowded spot, never close to
  another. The beachcomber adds to sandcastles, for the tide to take.
- Each pins at most one note a visit on the village noticeboard.
- Every few days one of them throws a little celebration: an event at a
  spot on their island, pinned on the village noticeboard, between 11am and
  7pm. They'll mention it to people they meet, and they're there on the day.
- `mute Bramble` works on them like anyone.

*Hints: "Somebody nearby is whistling to themself." "You hear someone
chatting away nearby, to nobody in particular."*

## Errands for the folk

Now and then one of the folk has lost something, and mentions it: "I've gone
and lost my silver thimble. Last I had it, I was among the trees near The
Duck Pond. If you happen across it, I'd be ever so glad." Saying hello to
them is the easiest way to hear about it, but they bring it up by themselves
sometimes too.

- It's lying where they said, and only you can see it there. Get within a
  couple of steps and something glints; on the spot, `look` shows it and
  `gather` picks it up.
- `give silver thimble to Bramble` hands it back, and they give you
  something for your home to say thank you: a painted pebble, a jar of
  honey, a tiny carved bird…
- `errand` reminds you what you're looking for, and where they've got to
  once you've found it.
- No hurry, and nothing to keep up with. One errand at a time, and a few
  hours before anyone asks you again. If you never get to it, it quietly
  goes when they row off, along with the thing they lost.
- What gets lost follows the season: a straw sunhat in summer, a red mitten
  in winter.

## Pieces that come together

Every day or so, something comes apart, its three pieces scattered about
one of the islands (mostly Home Island): an old music box, a torn treasure
map, or the season's own, like the great lantern for Lantern Night or the
snowman's finery for Snow Lights. Whoever's on hears where it belongs.

- Anyone can find the pieces. Something catches your eye when you're a
  couple of steps from one, and `gather` picks it up. You carry one piece at
  a time, so there's some left for everyone else.
- Take it to where it belongs and `place` it there. It waits, so nobody has
  to be on at the same time. `look` there shows what's been set down so far.
- When the last piece is in, it comes whole (the music box plays, the map's
  big red X turns out to be right there), everyone on hears about it,
  everyone who set a piece down gets a little keepsake, and anyone who
  wasn't on hears in their "since you were last here".
- If nobody gets to them, the pieces quietly fade after three days, from
  wherever they are, and nothing's said.

*Hint: "There's talk of something in pieces, scattered about this island.
The pieces must belong somewhere."*

## The sulking crab

While a status light on a noticeboard is down (see
[events-and-gatherings.md](events-and-gatherings.md)), a small crab sits on that
board and sulks. It can be `pat`ted, `fed`, `cheer`ed up, `comfort`ed, `hug`ged
and `tickle`d (it pinches, gently, as a warning). When the light comes back up,
the crab goes back to the shore.

## Making music together

Two people `hum`ming at the same spot are told they're making music together,
and (unless they're in a humming circle already) "(To keep in time, 'hum
along': the world counts you both in.)". `hum along` is also in `help music`.
A music box someone's taught a tune to plays it now and then when people are
near.

## Dark mode

`dark mode` doesn't change the colours. It makes the island quietly wrong, for
you alone: the fox is grinning, the ducks won't blink, the folk smile with
slightly too many teeth and wave with a sleeve where a hand should be, and the
scenery now and then says things like "You hear your own name, whispered, from
the long grass." Unsettling, never gory. `light mode` puts it all back (it says
so when you switch), and it's remembered between visits until you do.

Only the island's own words are touched: animals, the island folk, finds and
the scenery. Anything a person said, emoted, posted or wrote reaches you
exactly as they wrote it, and nobody else's island changes at all.

The hint: "For half a second the whole island flickers, like someone reaching
for a light switch. Somewhere, someone whispers about a dark mode." Once it's
on: "Something is very wrong with the animals today. Maybe it's time for light
mode."

## The typewriter

`make a typewriter` (any name with "typewriter" in it), then `type` beside it
(or `sit at the typewriter`). A page of an old island story opens over
everything, in the browser and `world.txt play`: each letter goes green when
it's right and red when it isn't, Backspace mends a mistake, and Esc gets up.
At the end of the page you get your words a minute, accuracy, how long it took
and your trickiest keys, and Enter winds in the next page.

The world remembers where you got to, so `type` carries on from there. `type
treasure island` (or `type alice`, `type toad`…) switches book, picking up
where you left off if it's the one you were on, and `type something else`
opens a random page of a random book. The books are Treasure Island, The Wind
in the Willows, Three Men in a Boat, Robinson Crusoe and Alice's Adventures in
Wonderland, all public domain, from Project Gutenberg. In Emacs the page comes
as plain text to read.

The hints: at your home's room, "A typewriter would sit nicely in a quiet
corner in here. Whole island stories to copy out, a page at a time." Beside
one: "A typewriter waits with a fresh sheet of paper in it. It's practically
begging to be typed on." It's also in the actions row (⌨️ type) when you're
standing by one.

## Hints at home

At your own home the scenery mentions the practical home things now and then:
paint ("A pot of paint sits by your door"), display cases ("An empty shelf by
the door is just waiting for a few of your finds"), and furniture ("Your home
could do with somewhere to sit"). Inside a building's room, the hints are about
describing and furnishing it. These are all in the help too (`help home`).
